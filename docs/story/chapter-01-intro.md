# Chapter 1 — Beat 0: Intro

Purpose: the script of the Chapter 1 intro — its frame, its cards, and the
hand-off into beat 1. Setting and history come from the
[story bible](README.md); the beat's place in the chapter is in the
[Chapter 1 outline](chapter-01.md). How the sequence is built belongs to the
OpenSpec change that implements it.

## Quick Scan

- The intro is **Absolute Connections Contractor Orientation, Module 1 of 14:
  *Where We Come From*** — a corporate video Joe's contract obliges him to
  watch. He sleeps through it.
- Its message: humanity faced **two dangers** — the Sun and anarchy — and the
  corporations **saved it twice**: by unifying the world, and by carrying out
  Helios's Plan for the Diaspora.
- Everything it says is true. Everything it says is slanted. It is the zaibatsu's
  official history, and the first unreliable narrator in the game.
- Presentation: 8-bit illustrated cards with captions over gloomy, mysterious
  chiptune music.
- It ends on Mars in 158 A.D., on a snore, and then on the ident of Module 2,
  *What's the Drill*, waiting for a key. Beat 1 opens with Joe switching it off.

## The Frame

The history is played straight; the packaging is corporate. The joke is who is
telling the story, never the story itself.

- **Cards 1, 2, 7 and 8 are serious.** The Brightening, the wars, the exodus
  and the people left behind are not undercut. The music carries them.
- **The corporate voice stays small**: the module count, a slogan, a phrase of
  corporate courtesy that lands colder than it means to. One touch per card at
  most, and none on the serious ones except card 8, where the courtesy is the
  chill.
- **The slant is in what is stressed and what is skipped.** Anarchy gets a
  card of its own; the corporations' price for order does not. Ganymede gets
  one line and a glitch.

## Presentation

- **Images:** one suggestive 8-bit illustration per card, low resolution, a
  limited palette. Silhouettes and light do the work; detail does not.
- **Movement:** it is a video game, so the cards are never still. Stars
  twinkle, lights flicker, the logo turns — the way 8-bit games moved, by
  cycling colours and turning sprites rather than redrawing the picture.
- **Captions:** short, typed out under the image, on the same screen and in
  the same pixels: a pixel font of our own, with no italics, so the script's
  italics are shown in yellow. The idents set their title on two lines.
- **Music:** gloomy and mysterious chiptune, one loop for the whole video. It
  drops out twice: at the Ganymede glitch and at the end. It is also
  **Absolute Connections' motif** — see [The Motif](#the-motif).
- The video opens on the corporate ident with **PRESS ANY KEY TO BEGIN
  ORIENTATION** — the corporation makes you opt in to its own propaganda. It
  closes on the same ident for the next module, with **PRESS ANY KEY TO
  CONTINUE ORIENTATION**: one module down, thirteen to go.
- Skipping is allowed, and the skip prompt reads **Skipping is recorded.**

## The Ganymede Glitch

Card 6 is the one place the video visibly hides something, and it is the
intro's clue.

- The caption reads only **"Ganymede was found unsuitable."** — the official
  line, nothing more. The script never explains, contradicts or hints in words.
- On that line the picture **glitches for two frames** and the **music drops
  out**, then the video carries on as if nothing happened.
- A first-time player notices *that* something is wrong, not *what*. Nothing
  in Chapter 1 pays it off; see the bible's open questions on Ganymede.

## Hand-off to Beat 1

The intro ends on card 11; beat 1 begins in Joe's room on the Mars surface,
with Module 2's ident waiting on his screen: **PRESS ANY KEY TO CONTINUE
ORIENTATION**. He presses one, by slapping the screen off:

> **JOE:** F@#K1N6 corporate propaganda… I can't stand it.

The line is spelled that way on purpose. It is the in-world profanity filter at
work — the corporations censor even their own contractor's complaint — which
keeps the joke inside the world rather than winking at the player.

## The Motif

The orientation's music is the corporation's theme. Wherever Absolute
Connections is present, the motif returns — first in the intro, then in beat 6,
in the offices where Danny Mellow waits. The player hears the corporation before
they meet it, and learns to associate that gloomy, mysterious loop with it.

## What the Intro Must Not Reveal

- The parasite, the invaders, or Danny Mellow.
- Why Joe matters. The video addresses "contractors like you"; it never names
  him.
- Anything about Ganymede beyond the official verdict.

## Open Questions

- Modules 2 to 14: a running gag later in the game, or never mentioned again?
- Whether skipping really is recorded — a line that could pay off later, or
  stay a joke.
